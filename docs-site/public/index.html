How Equinox works, end to end

Perpetual futures on tokenized assets. Orders match in a MagicBlock Ephemeral Rollup in milliseconds, prices are verified on Solana, and USDC never leaves the program vault on Solana.

01Overview

Three products share one program and one USDC mint: stock perps (TSLA), pre-IPO perps (OpenAI, SpaceX, Anthropic) and a launchpad whose graduated tokens can become perps.

The split is deliberate. Everything that has to be fast (placing, cancelling and matching orders, margin checks, funding, liquidation) runs in the rollup against a delegated copy of the market. Everything that has to be safe (the USDC, the verified prices, the final state) lives on Solana L1. The rollup commits its state back to L1 on a schedule, and custody moves between the two only through explicit inbox and outbox receipts.

02Where everything runs

Four runtimes, placed so the latency-sensitive hop (market maker to rollup) is about 2 ms.

03Market accounts

Each market is a core account plus 26 children. The whole 27-account bundle is delegated to the rollup; custody and prices stay on L1.

All four markets share one exchange and one test-USDC mint, so the same USDC works everywhere. Margin is isolated: a trader has one seat per market.

04Onboarding

One wallet signature, then everything is signed by an in-app trading key. "Start trading" funds, seats and deposits in one go.

Devnet trade-off: the trading key is kept in the browser's local storage. A production deployment would use embedded wallets or scoped session keys. Withdrawals always pay out to the connected wallet, never to the trading key.

05Order lifecycle

The order path is pre-warmed (delegation status, blockhash, price age), so a click only signs and sends.

Order types: limit and market (IOC), post-only, reduce-only, with a self-trade policy. Replace (33) moves a resting order in one instruction, which is how the market maker requotes without leaving gaps.

07Prices and oracles

Every market has one L1 OracleSnapshotV3. Two ways write it: a verified Pyth update, or a bounded price reporter.

Rules the program enforces

  • Orders need a price at most 10 seconds old and an open session. Pyth's OverNight session trades; Closed (weekends, US holidays) refuses new orders.
  • A reporter post may move the price by at most 0.5% plus 0.1% per elapsed second, capped at 10% per post; the reporter walks large moves in over several posts.
  • Pyth-priced markets have no reporter and refuse reporter posts. The reporter key is named per market by the authority (66).

08Margin, funding, liquidation

  • Initial margin is 20% (5× maximum); margin is isolated per market seat.
  • Funding accrues from the gap between the book and the oracle, capped by elapsed time; the program only lets the accumulator move forward.
  • Liquidation uses the same risk code as order placement, so the keeper can't liquidate a healthy seat.

09Commits to Solana

The rollup's state becomes Solana state at each commit. Trading pauses for about 1.2 seconds while the bundle is written back.

10Withdrawals

Two steps: a risk-checked request in the rollup, then a claim on L1 that pays only the seat owner's USDC account.

11Pre-IPO and baskets

OPENAI, SPACEX and ANTHROPIC perps priced from PreStocks tokens. A basket buys several legs with one click.

12Launchpad to perp

Stock-themed tokens launch on Meteora Dynamic Bonding Curves priced in USDC. A graduated token can be listed as an Equinox perp.

13Market-maker service

One Rust process on the Singapore VM, about 2 ms from the rollup, running every market in the deployment manifest.

18Trust model and limits

Known limits

  • Taking a market out of the rollup (undelegation) is blocked by the deployed MagicBlock delegation program; it isn't needed for trading, custody or commits.
  • No L1 escape hatch yet if the rollup were permanently down. Funds stay in the L1 vault.
  • Pre-IPO and launched-token prices come from a bounded reporter key, not a signed oracle network.
  • Devnet only: test USDC, not real funds.
